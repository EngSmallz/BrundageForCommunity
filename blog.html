Stop the Misinformation: Read the Initiatives

By Justin Brundage • Concrete School Board Director

I am getting increasingly frustrated with the amount of misinformation and partisan rhetoric surrounding Initiative 26-001 and Initiative 26-638. People are free to oppose these initiatives. They are free to support them. We should be able to have an honest discussion about what they actually do. But that discussion needs to start with one simple thing: read the actual initiatives.

Do not rely on a Facebook post. Do not rely on a campaign flyer. Do not rely on a partisan talking point. Read the language yourself and decide what you think.

I support both of these initiatives. But supporting them does not mean we should exaggerate what they do. In fact, I think our arguments are stronger when we stick to the actual text.

INITIATIVE 26-638

One claim I have seen is that this initiative will require students to undergo invasive physical examinations to play sports. That is not what the initiative says.

IL26-638 uses the routine sports physical examination and adds a requirement that a health care provider verify biological sex using specified criteria, including reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or naturally produced testosterone levels.

People can debate whether that requirement is appropriate. But describing it as simply creating a new invasive examination does not accurately describe the text.

I have also seen claims that the initiative contains no penalties. That is not accurate either. The initiative does allow athletic organizations to impose penalties for violations involving students, coaches, and school administrators. It also provides for proportional penalties and an appeal process. It does not establish one specific automatic fine for every violation.

Those details matter.

INITIATIVE 26-001

There has also been a lot of discussion about IL26-001 and school counseling records.

The initiative gives parents broad rights to inspect and receive copies of their child's school records, including medical records and mental health counseling records. It establishes a 10 business day timeline for providing those records.

But there is an important exception that I think gets left out of some of the discussion.

The initiative specifically says a school would not be required to release a student's medical, health, or mental health counseling records to a parent while there is a pending investigation of child abuse or neglect conducted by law enforcement or DCYF when that parent is the target of the investigation, unless the parent obtains a court order.

THE BOTTOM LINE

I support these initiatives, and I am comfortable saying that openly. But I also believe supporters should be willing to acknowledge what is actually in the text, just as opponents should be willing to do the same.

If there is a provision you disagree with, point to it. If you believe something will have a particular consequence, explain why and show people the language that leads you there.

There is plenty to debate in both initiatives. We do not need to exaggerate them, misrepresent them, or repeat something simply because we saw it on social media.

Read the initiatives. Read the protections. Read the exceptions. Read what actually changes. Then decide for yourself.

That is the conversation I would like to see.
